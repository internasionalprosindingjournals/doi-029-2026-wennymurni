THE EFFECT OF PROLANIS SERVICES PLUS HOME VISITS ON THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF PATIENTS WITH DIABETES MELLITUS IN EAST JAKARTA
Weny Murni
Master of Nursing Study Program, Faculty of Nursing and Health Sciences, Universitas Muhammadiyah Semarang, Indonesia

DOI: https://doi.org/10.31004/029-2026
Keywords: diabetes mellitus; Prolanis; home visits; quality of life; primary healthcare

Abstract

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic disease that requires continuous management and may affect patients' quality of life through physical, psychological, social, and environmental dimensions. Diabetes mellitus comprises several types, including type 1 diabetes mellitus, type 2 diabetes mellitus, gestational diabetes mellitus, and diabetes due to other specific causes, each with different characteristics and management needs. Nevertheless, all patients with diabetes require continuous monitoring, education, self-management, and support to prevent complications and maintain quality of life. The Chronic Disease Management Program (Prolanis) is one strategy implemented in primary healthcare to support long-term management of chronic diseases. However, routine Prolanis services may not fully address the individual needs of patients with diabetes. This study aimed to analyze the effect of Prolanis services strengthened by structured home visits on the quality of life of patients with diabetes mellitus in the working area of Jatinegara Health Center, East Jakarta. The study was conducted in two stages: development of a structured home-visit module using a research and development approach, followed by a quasi-experimental pre-test post-test control group study. The study involved 60 respondents who, in the actual study, were patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus. Respondents were divided into an intervention group (n=30) and a control group (n=30) and followed for eight weeks. The intervention group received routine Prolanis services plus four structured home visits, while the control group received routine Prolanis services. Quality of life was measured using the WHOQOL-BREF and analyzed using Wilcoxon and Mann–Whitney tests. The results showed no significant baseline difference between groups (p=0.818). After intervention, both groups showed significant improvement (p<0.001). The intervention group increased from 74.73 to 96.10 (Δ=21.37), while the control group increased from 73.07 to 90.47 (Δ=17.40). The between-group difference was significant (p=0.010). These findings indicate that structured home visits may strengthen routine Prolanis services and improve quality of life among patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus. Further studies are needed before generalizing the findings to other diabetes types.
